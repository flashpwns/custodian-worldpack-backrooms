## Labeling rules (the game owner's ratified policy — follow exactly)

Addressee (who must answer):
- "explicit": the line names who it is for (vocative "Tonya, ..." / "..., Malcolm?" / "Hey Giselle", or a name list). addressees = those names.
- "group": collective address language for everyone present ("everyone", "all of you", "you all", "y'all", "guys", "anyone"/"anybody", "you three", "folks", "team", "does anyone know", "which of you"). addressees = all three.
- "subset": a quantified or named part of the group that is not a plain vocative ("you two" when context makes clear which two, "the rest of you" after one person answered, "everyone except Tonya"). addressees = those people.
- "untargeted": a fresh shared question or remark with NO address language ("Where are we going?", "what's next", "Well this seems safe."). addressees = [].
- "inherited": no address language, but a genuine follow-up, repair or continuation whose responder is set by the conversation. Owner-ratified responder priority: explicit addressee > target of a conversational repair > owner of the semantic antecedent / last substantive speaker ("Why?", "Since when?", an echo of their words) > target of the active conversational activity > a knowledgeable responder > fairness rotation (fresh untargeted shared questions only). A genuine follow-up is NOT rotated. addressees = the inherited responder(s).

Speech acts and relations:
- "question" covers all questions (use question_form). A polite "Can you tell me about yourself?" is "request" (predicate person.self_description). "Introduce yourself" is "request".
- A repair of who was addressed or which question was asked is "repair"; predicate = that of the ORIGINAL request; addressees = the corrected target(s) ("explicit" if named, else "inherited"); relation "repair".
- An elliptical line ("How about you two?", "Your turn", "And Malcolm?") is "elliptical_continuation"; predicate inherited from the prior request/activity; relation "continuation".
- A genuine follow-up to the person who just answered ("Why?", "Since when?") is "question", relation "continuation", addressee "inherited". A follow-up about the time, duration or degree of what was just answered keeps the PREDICATE of the question that person was answering; a follow-up asking the reason is conversation.explanation; asking how they know is conversation.basis_of. Corrections of the addressee, of the thing meant or of the aspect asked are "repair" and keep (or, for an aspect correction, change to) the predicate the player now means.
