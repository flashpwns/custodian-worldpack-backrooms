## Authority and interpretation

This baseline reconciles the Gameplay Constitution, Vision-Centric Roadmap, Simulation Doctrine, Design Charter, Phase 01A ledger, Phase 01B challenge, surviving audits, current source, current tests, and the human-ratified Phase 01C product decisions.

The Gameplay Constitution defines current Yellow Beast player/product intent. Simulation Doctrine defines setting-independent Custodian reality law and now explicitly permits the Yellow Beast specialization. `canon/SOURCE_POLICY.md` plus admitted claim records independently govern canon. This baseline defines present implementation truth, and the Vision Pass Map defines the current campaign. Historical roadmaps, reconciliation audits, and old implementation-state entries remain evidence only; they do not prove current completeness.

The following owner decisions are current product authority and are explicitly reconciled by V01 in Simulation Doctrine, the Gameplay Constitution, and the Design Charter. Their runtime implementation remains deferred to the owning later passes:

- player death ends an employee career, not necessarily the persistent world;
- the player may restore the sanctioned pre-expedition checkpoint or begin a new career in the same world;
- the restore is an explicit metagame branch abandonment, not in-world time travel;
- player-controlled saves exist in ASYNC, before entry, and after return, but not as ordinary mid-expedition slots;
- invisible transactional crash recovery is required and is not a player save;
- new worlds begin in 1991 and deterministic, auditable real-elapsed-time institutional progression is intended;
- offline simulation must not routinely consume meaningful player-facing opportunities;
- personnel state is canonical;
- internal entity identity must remain unavailable to player and institutional observers until a legitimate naming history exists;
- Still Life and Bacteria production behavior requires source-admission reconciliation rather than deletion by default or unsupported canon claims.

V01 resolves the former Doctrine §§19.13–19.17 / Laws XL–XLII and Design Charter §§6 / 55.6 authority conflicts and records the explicit bounded elapsed-time specialization contemplated by Doctrine §§2.9 and 21.10–21.11. The unchanged retirement, autosave, replay, provider, and action-driven runtime remains implementation evidence and known defect, not contrary product authority.

## Executive baseline

Yellow Beast today is a launchable Windows Electron application containing a substantial, deterministic, action-driven Clear-Q4 field-operations vertical slice. It has real canonical authorities for bounded geography, equipment custody, mission state, communications delivery, observer projections, evidence, institutional records, assignments, environment, personnel continuity scaffolding, rare phenomenon records, outcomes, and persistence.

It is not the persistent, continuously simulated 1990s ASYNC workplace promised by the Gameplay Constitution.

The ordinary production experience is a world library, name-only employee record, compressed preparation/deployment, STANDARD radio procedure, structured or bounded natural field actions, LOCAL/STANDARD communication, persistent exploration, evidence and equipment handling, return/debrief, and explicit advancement to another assignment. Canonical activity advances when a player/service call invokes an operational cycle or between-operation processor. The world does not presently continue on an independent canonical scheduler.

The most accurate product classification is:

> A technically playable, action-driven Clear-Q4 vertical slice with valuable domain algorithms and serious trust defects at persistence, ownership, lifecycle recovery, provider knowledge boundaries, and verification aggregation.

Historical “completed pass,” “playable beta,” and green `npm test` labels must not be used as broader claims.

- Windows world library, named-world creation, world reopen, rename, import/export, delete confirmation, settings, and isolated application-data storage.
- First/last-name ASYNC personnel creation and confirmation.
- Clear-Q4 briefing with assigned work order, team, objectives, reporting requirement, risks, and issued equipment.

- Observer-safe current-location description, known routes, map fragments, objectives, team/contact state, equipment, communications, evidence, and return state.
- Structured field actions for supported movement and object/mission operations.
- Bounded natural-language intent for simple supported actions; unsupported or ambiguous attempts can fail without mutation.
- Player-authored LOCAL and STANDARD messages, with attempted transmission distinct from delivery/acknowledgment.
- Nearby-worker orders represented as persistent tasks with accept, delay, refuse, unheard, and clarification outcomes.
- Persistent bounded geography expansion and revisits through the Survey Frontier.
- Equipment identity, custody, transfer, use, loss, condition, containers, and reconciliation.

## Determinism status

### Deterministic action resolution

Substantial current domain resolution is seeded, ordered, and repeatable. Structured actions, bounded procedural geography, assignment choice, personnel tendencies, environment initialization, communication transitions, and rare materialization have useful deterministic contracts. The y33 workload reports `deterministic: true`.

This does not establish continuous simulation determinism because no continuous scheduler exists.

### Canonical save/reload equivalence

Broken on first normalization. The reproduced long-world mismatch is one-time schema/default insertion; repeated normalized loads were stable. The contract still fails because load changes canonical representation without the save writer committing that normalized representation.

### Personnel persistence

Broken. Current character rebuilding ignores Q4 condition events written by `persistSession`. This is genuine state loss and duplicated ownership, not a cosmetic record difference.

### Migration/default normalization

Broken as a neutral serialization contract. `saveCanonical` validates a migrated temporary load but commits the original unnormalized temporary file. Migration is therefore delayed until a later read and changes the canonical in-memory shape.

### Backup restoration

Broken for lifecycle integrity. A technically valid previous-good file can be semantically illegal after a later retirement transaction. File validity alone is insufficient recovery authority.

### Player checkpoint semantics

Missing. There is no legislated checkpoint object distinct from canonical world/session data. Existing per-action autosaves are crash/session persistence, not the sanctioned player checkpoint design.

### Crash recovery semantics

Partially implemented but conflated. Temporary writes, previous-good files, damaged-primary preservation, and session backups are useful. Recovery currently lacks a transaction/journal model and semantic lifecycle validation, and save performs simulation work.

## Persistence status

Persistence cannot be described as generically “working” or “broken.”

- Stable IDs, many domain snapshots, short-run resume, atomic file replacement, previous-good retention, and isolated application profiles work.
- First-load normalized equivalence fails.
- Personnel condition replay fails.
- Previous-good restoration violates retirement.
- Session/world/domain copies are reconciled during save.
- Player checkpoint and crash recovery semantics do not exist as separate authorities.
- No safe basis exists yet for layering continuous simulation, deep personnel history, long offline catch-up, or multi-career succession onto the current transaction model.

## Current launchability

**Does it launch?** Yes, on the current verified Windows environment and artifact.

**Is it technically playable?** Yes. A bounded Clear-Q4 field-operation loop is technically playable, though major human-facing and causal paths remain incomplete or unverified.

**Does it satisfy the Gameplay Constitution?** No. Continuous simulation, time, onboarding, facility life, deep personnel/career progression, broad ACTION, offline progression, multi-career worlds, audio, and complete presentation are absent; several trust boundaries are broken.

**Is it safe to expand without foundation repair?** No. Test aggregation, serialization normalization, personnel ownership/replay, lifecycle recovery, provider boundaries, and duplicated canonical ownership must be repaired before dependent simulation expansion.

## Highest-risk architecture

1. **Save-time canonical reconciliation.** `persistSession` owns too many mutations. A scheduler would multiply race/order/replay failures if introduced before transaction separation.
2. **Duplicated personnel state.** World characters, run team members, continuity records, and mismatched events already lose condition state.
3. **Lifecycle versus recovery.** Current/previous-good validity lacks a monotonic lifecycle transaction or explicit metagame checkpoint authority.
4. **Distributed institutional cognition.** Communication, Standard, evidence, survey, institutional response, and world knowledge stores can diverge under autonomous processing.
5. **Duplicated geography.** Current run/world snapshots and legacy procedural state can become competing truth under long-horizon mutation.
6. **Provider authority overlay.** Observer-safe context is undermined by later inclusion of internal doctrine/design/domain text.
7. **False-green verification.** Large-scale changes can appear safe because the official aggregate omits current and failing authorities.
8. **Unadmitted phenomenon behavior.** Production expansion risks presenting developer intent as Kane-primary truth or deleting desired design before source review.
