# YELLOW BEAST GAMEPLAY CONSTITUTION
## Player Promise and Runtime Experience Authority

**Status:** Human-ratified Yellow Beast 1.0 product constitution
**Ratified:** Phase 01C, 2026-08-12
**V01 authority effect:** Active Yellow Beast constitutional specialization; implementation remains governed by the approved current pass.

## 0. Authority and Scope

This document defines the current Yellow Beast player promise and product intent. It is the Yellow Beast worldpack/product constitutional addendum contemplated by Simulation Doctrine §§21.10–21.11.

The authority stack is:

1. `SIMULATION_DOCTRINE.md` defines setting-independent Custodian reality, causality, persistence, observer, and provider law.
2. This Gameplay Constitution explicitly specializes Yellow Beast lifecycle, checkpoint, and elapsed-time defaults without weakening those universal protections.
3. `canon/SOURCE_POLICY.md` and admitted claim/admission records independently determine what external Kane material may be treated as canon. Product intent and implemented behavior are not canon admission.
4. `docs/YELLOW_BEAST_1.0_DESIGN_CHARTER.md` elaborates this product destination and must conform to this Constitution.
5. `docs/YELLOW_BEAST_CURRENT_BASELINE.md` describes current implementation truth; it does not redefine the product.
6. `docs/YELLOW_BEAST_VISION_PASS_MAP.md` defines the current implementation campaign and pass scope.
7. `docs/IMPLEMENTATION_STATE.md` provides present orientation and historical implementation evidence; historical pass completion is not product completeness.

The old numbered Implementation Roadmap, Codex Execution Map, reconciliation audits, recovery prompts, and pass summaries are historical planning/evidence only.

### Explicit Yellow Beast specialization

Yellow Beast intentionally:

- models deterministic real-elapsed institutional progression while the player is absent;
- ends the deceased employee's career without automatically retiring the persistent world;
- permits a later employee career in that same world;
- permits player checkpoints only in ASYNC, before Complex entry, and after return;
- permits invisible transactional crash recovery without making it a player save;
- permits a sanctioned pre-expedition metagame reload that abandons the failed expedition branch and is not in-world time travel.

These rules are Yellow Beast-specific. They do not become universal Custodian defaults.

### 1. Player Promise

Yellow Beast places the player in the role of an ordinary ASYNC employee beginning a career in 1991.

The player is not the protagonist of a generated story and does not control the world. They are one employee inside a persistent institutional and physical simulation.

The player works assignments, enters the Complex with coworkers, explores unknown architecture, communicates with STANDARD and their local team, gathers evidence, develops professional relationships, succeeds, fails, survives, disappears, or dies.

Custodian determines what actually occurs.

The player may attempt anything physically or socially reasonable through the interfaces available to them, but the simulation is never obligated to make the attempt successful.

The world continues to exist beyond the player.

The intended experience is not being told a Backrooms story.

**The intended experience is discovering what happens when you have a job inside one.**

---

## 2. The Fantasy

Yellow Beast should feel simultaneously like:

- a 1990s internship;
- an alternate-reality exploration simulator;
- an institutional bureaucracy;
- a persistent workplace filled with actual people;
- and a horror simulation based specifically on Kane Parsons' Backrooms canon.

The mundane and extraordinary must coexist.

An expedition may involve an impossible humanoid creature pursuing the team through unstable architecture.

Another expedition may involve six hours of successfully documenting fluorescent hallways.

Both are valid Yellow Beast.

The simulation does not manufacture spectacle merely because the player is bored.

---

## 3. First Five Minutes

Every new career begins with employee onboarding.

The player selects:

- first name;
- last name;
- male or female;
- height;
- weight;
- a short prose description of previous work experience.

The work-experience description is silently interpreted into hidden personnel competencies. These competencies are never shown numerically to the player.

The onboarding sequence then proceeds through semi-interactive cinematic presentation.

1. The player receives an ASYNC employment letter addressed personally to them.
2. The letter contains both congratulations and extensive corporate liability disclaimers.
3. The letter is folded away.
4. A projector starts.
5. ASYNC's canonical promotional video plays, explaining basic Complex expedition procedures.
6. The projector shuts off.
7. Personnel brief the player on their randomly selected first assignment.
8. The player enters staging.
9. Assigned equipment is shown.
10. The player may request additional equipment.
11. ASYNC may approve or deny those requests.
12. The player may review their coworkers' personnel files.
13. The player finalizes the loadout.
14. The expedition enters the Threshold room.
15. The team approaches KV31.
16. The player crosses.
17. The Outpost steel door closes behind them.
18. The complete gameplay interface is revealed.

The game has now begun.

Future careers in the same world may skip previously experienced onboarding material.

## 4. Fundamental Runtime Model

Yellow Beast is not conventionally turn-based.

The simulation operates continuously.

The player controls their own character through discrete ACTION submissions.

Therefore:

**The world clock does not wait for the player.
The player's ACTION resolver does.**

Between ACTION submissions:

- clocks may advance;
- radio traffic may continue;
- NPCs may communicate;
- NPCs may make decisions;
- background simulation may proceed;
- environmental processes may occur;
- remote events may occur;
- Complex phenomena may evolve.

Continuous operation does not authorize host timers, renderer cadence, provider latency, or uncontrolled wall-clock callbacks to mutate canonical state. Real elapsed time establishes bounded eligibility. Custodian's deterministic scheduler orders and resolves canonical events, persists their results, and must reproduce the same history from the same canonical state, seed, and eligible elapsed duration.

A new player ACTION creates a discrete resolution point for the player character.

The primary gameplay rhythm is:

**Player-visible surroundings
→ communications / equipment / evidence review
→ player ACTION
→ Custodian resolution
→ interpreter portrayal
→ updated surroundings**

The interpreter portrays reality.

It does not create reality.

---

## 5. The ACTION Interface

ACTION represents what the player character personally attempts to do within physical space.

Examples:

- follow the guideline toward Room 14A;
- inspect the missing ceiling tile;
- pick up the flashlight;
- photograph the doorway;
- run toward the Outpost;
- toss a glove through an opening;
- climb onto a table;
- hide behind a wall;
- retrace the team's route.

The player may attempt actions that have never been explicitly programmed as named verbs.

The interpreter translates intent into a form Custodian can adjudicate.

Custodian then determines the result according to:

- physical state;
- geography;
- personnel ability;
- equipment;
- environmental state;
- Complex phenomena;
- hidden information;
- established canon;
- and other simulation state.

A player's wording never grants them capabilities they do not possess.

“I sprint one hundred meters in six seconds” is an attempt, not a fact.

“I identify the unknown compound” is an attempt, not knowledge.

“I perform emergency surgery” does not create medical competence.

---

## 6. Communication

Dialogue is never entered through ACTION.

There are two communication channels.

### LOCAL

LOCAL represents speech or local communications available to expedition personnel within established communications range.

All applicable coworkers may hear LOCAL communication.

Not everybody should respond.

Responses depend on:

- whether they heard it;
- whether a response is warranted;
- personality;
- relationships;
- authority;
- workload;
- current activity;
- emotional state;
- knowledge;
- and other simulated factors.

LOCAL may be used to request that another person perform an action.

Examples:

“Bennett, check that doorway.”

“Nora, grab a photograph.”

“Steven, stay here.”

These are requests, not direct control over another character.

NPCs may comply, refuse, misunderstand, hesitate, partially comply, or independently choose another response.

### STANDARD

STANDARD communicates with appropriate ASYNC personnel outside the Complex.

STANDARD may:

- provide assignment direction;
- request evidence;
- conduct radio checks;
- clarify institutional information;
- record reports;
- provide advice;
- misunderstand the team;
- possess incomplete information;
- or occasionally possess incorrect information.

STANDARD does not possess omniscient access to Custodian.

ASYNC is an actor inside the simulation, not an interface to objective truth.

---

## 7. NPC Action Scheduling

Requests made through LOCAL must never deadlock gameplay.

When the player requests a physical action from another person, Custodian creates an NPC intent rather than requiring the player to surrender control.

Example:

Player LOCAL:
“Nora, check the doorway.”

Nora may answer:
“Yeah. Give me a second.”

Custodian then schedules Nora's attempted action.

The player remains free to submit their own ACTION.

NPC physical actions and player physical actions may therefore overlap.

During subsequent simulation advancement, Custodian resolves actions according to their duration and circumstances.

The next interpreter update reports only events the player could reasonably perceive.

Therefore:

**communication can initiate NPC behavior without converting dialogue into a second ACTION system.**

---

## 8. Time

New worlds begin in 1991.

Outside the Complex, calendar days advance according to real-world elapsed time.

Inside the Complex, expedition duration is tracked using Complex elapsed time.

Complex time need not correspond predictably to outside time.

Standard/institutional chronology and Complex elapsed time are distinct canonical domains. One must never silently overwrite or stand in for the other.

Wall-clock readings never directly change the world. They are validated into elapsed-time eligibility, which Custodian advances through deterministic, bounded, auditable scheduling. Clock rollback, timezone changes, long absences, restart cadence, and catch-up batching must not change the resulting canonical ordering.

Long travel may therefore have substantial consequences.

A player may request a long traversal rather than submitting movement one room at a time.

Custodian may compress uneventful movement.

However, elapsed time still matters.

A twelve-hour traversal may cause:

- missed radio checks;
- personnel reactions;
- assignment consequences;
- fatigue or situational effects;
- institutional concern;
- or unrelated world changes.

Traversal must be interruptible by meaningful events.

---

## 9. Pause and Idle Control

The player may pause and resume active simulation using an explicit control such as the spacebar.

When simulation is running and the player remains idle for a sufficient period, the player character gradually becomes an autonomous simulated person.

The character may perform reasonable idle activities such as:

- surveying;
- watching coworkers;
- inspecting surroundings;
- waiting;
- resting;
- or maintaining equipment.

After approximately five minutes of real idle time, the player character may operate using the same underlying autonomous-personnel systems as coworkers.

The player may immediately reclaim direct control through clear input such as mouse movement.

The simulation must never secretly perform dramatic or highly consequential player-character decisions merely because the user stepped away.

---

## 10. Perception and Interpreter Perspective

The interpreter always addresses the player in second person.

Example:

“You enter Room 14A.”

Descriptions are limited to information reasonably available from the player character's perspective.

Yellow Beast should portray:

- visible architecture;
- audible phenomena;
- obvious environmental conditions;
- visible coworker behavior;
- immediately perceptible objects;
- and other sensory information.

The interpreter should not ordinarily dictate the player's thoughts or emotions.

Prefer:

“The fluorescent hum has become noticeably irregular. Bennett has stopped speaking.”

over:

“You feel afraid.”

The player decides how their character feels.

Descriptions may be extremely detailed when space and circumstances warrant it.

Detail should build place, navigation, memory, and unease rather than become decorative prose.

---

## 11. Geography

The Complex is persistent, procedural, modular, and unstable.

The map begins primarily from known areas surrounding the Complex-side Outpost and expands through exploration.

New spaces may be procedurally generated within canonical negative space.

Architecture may:

- change;
- contradict earlier surveys;
- contain missing or new doors;
- create unexpected pits;
- reconnect differently;
- disagree with maps;
- or otherwise behave according to established Kane-adjacent rules.

The map must be capable of reflecting these changes.

Geographic contradiction is not automatically a bug.

Deterministic persistence must nevertheless preserve Custodian's actual world state correctly.

---

## 12. Evidence and Institutional Knowledge

A verbal description to STANDARD begins the evidence process.

STANDARD may then request:

- photographs;
- video;
- measurements;
- recordings;
- samples;
- witness confirmation;
- retrieval;
- or other documentation.

Information that does not return from the Complex may still enter ASYNC records as testimony.

Testimony without supporting evidence remains appropriately uncertain.

Example:

“Personnel reported sight of a table walking as if it was a crab.”

If no evidence returned:

“Concept remains speculative as of [date].”

Institutional certainty must depend upon the provenance and quality of evidence.

The player's discoveries become part of persistent ASYNC knowledge.

Information may later affect:

- assignments;
- maps;
- briefing materials;
- reports;
- institutional theories;
- and future careers.

---

## 13. Equipment

Every expedition receives an assigned loadout.

The player may request additional equipment during staging.

Approval depends on simulated institutional factors including prior performance and standing.

Equipment management should remain lightweight.

Objects may:

- be carried;
- passed between personnel;
- dropped;
- lost;
- damaged;
- altered;
- or affected by phenomena.

Some durability and failure simulation is desirable.

Yellow Beast should not become a storage-management game.

---

## 14. Assignments

Assignments should ultimately originate from Custodian's simulated institutional needs and knowledge.

Assignments may arise from:

- unexplored mapped territory;
- earlier player discoveries;
- NPC discoveries;
- unresolved evidence;
- known anomalies;
- mapping requirements;
- recovery operations;
- documentation needs;
- or other ASYNC objectives.

Procedurally generated missions are preferred.

Preset assignment templates are acceptable as scaffolding where necessary, provided their contents are instantiated dynamically from world state.

The eventual target is that the institution creates work because the simulated institution has work that needs doing.

---

## 15. Mission Freedom

Assignments are objectives, not invisible walls.

The player may ignore them.

The Complex does not prevent unauthorized exploration because the mission briefing said otherwise.

Coworkers and STANDARD may react.

Mission failure may produce:

- negative evaluations;
- pay deductions described through flavor text;
- reduced trust;
- decreased equipment-request approval;
- reduced institutional standing;
- or other bureaucratic consequences.

Conversely, disciplined successful performance improves standing and future privileges.

Unexpected discoveries may legitimately justify abandoning an original assignment.

---

## 16. Career Progression

Yellow Beast may use an underlying level-like career progression system.

It must never present itself as conventional RPG XP.

Progression represents professional tenure, experience, reliability, and institutional standing.

Successful expeditions contribute to the employee's career record.

Every completed expedition generates an ASYNC-style performance evaluation.

Career progression may affect:

- equipment approval;
- treatment by STANDARD;
- access;
- assignment type;
- staffing privileges;
- ability to request specific personnel;
- institutional trust;
- and future opportunities.

The player should experience progression through bureaucracy, not floating numbers.

---

## 17. Personnel

Coworkers are people.

Every generated employee possesses persistent hidden characteristics that may include:

- competencies;
- personality;
- relationships;
- memories;
- fears;
- preferences;
- authority;
- career history;
- knowledge;
- emotional state;
- and other behavior-driving information.

Whenever a personnel field can affect simulation resolution or future institutional causality, it is canonical unless an owning authority explicitly documents it as derived presentation state. This includes condition, history, competencies, relationships, career state, memories, injuries, assignments, and institutional consequences where modeled.

Closing, loading, replaying, rebuilding projections, or changing presentation/provider systems must not discard that canonical personnel state.

These values are not exposed as game statistics.

Personnel may:

- become friends;
- become enemies;
- trust the player;
- distrust the player;
- refuse requests;
- request assignments;
- seek promotion;
- receive promotion;
- be disciplined;
- quit;
- retire;
- disappear;
- become injured;
- die;
- or change while the player is offline.

Shared experiences should be a primary driver of relationships.

NPCs should reference previous expeditions, including expeditions and events that occurred while the user was offline.

---

## 18. Staffing

Expedition teams are normally assigned procedurally with light balancing.

Higher institutional standing may eventually grant privileges such as requesting specific personnel.

Such requests remain subject to ASYNC approval.

The player is not always expedition leader.

Authority matters.

When another person leads the expedition, the player does not acquire command authority merely by being human-controlled.

---

## 19. ASYNC Facility

Between expeditions, the player may navigate ASYNC through a spatially represented clickable facility interface.

Areas may include:

- briefing;
- staging;
- biopsy;
- document access;
- personnel systems;
- other institutional departments.

Facility state should feel alive.

A room may occasionally be unavailable because something else is happening.

Example:

The player clicks the briefing room and is turned away because a classified briefing is underway.

The facility is not required to become a second free-roaming ACTION-based simulation for 1.0.

Its purpose is to spatialize the institution and make ASYNC feel inhabited rather than reduce downtime to disconnected menus.

---

## 20. Offline World Simulation

The world does not freeze merely because the player closes the game.

Offline progression may produce substantial consequences.

Those consequences arise only from Custodian-owned deterministic scheduling over validated elapsed-time eligibility. They require canonical causes, stable event ordering, transactional persistence, observer-safe knowledge propagation, and auditable provenance. The scheduler may not use uncontrolled timers or presentation systems as mutation authority.

When the player returns:

- expeditions may have occurred;
- personnel may have changed;
- discoveries may have been made;
- management may have changed;
- funding may have changed;
- coworkers may be missing;
- ASYNC may have undergone serious institutional events;
- or extremely unusual temporal circumstances may have occurred.

Offline change is allowed to be significant enough that an old world becomes undesirable or difficult to continue.

Starting a new world is an acceptable player decision.

The world should continue without the player.

It must not merely complete every interesting objective while the player is absent.

Offline simulation should generate history and consequences, not play the player's career for them.

Opportunity protection is a binding scheduler constraint. Offline processing must reserve, defer, transform, or otherwise preserve a meaningful share of important exploration, relationship, evidence, assignment, and phenomenon opportunities for player participation. It may change or close particular opportunities when canonically justified; it may not systematically exhaust the product's most meaningful work during absence.

---

## 21. Death

Yellow Beast provides no gameplay protection against death after onboarding and interface comprehension have been established.

When the player character dies:

**the gameplay UI immediately shuts off like a 1990s CRT television.**

No explanation is given.

No conventional death screen appears.

The player is then shown ASYNC's public cover story falsifying the circumstances of the employee's death.

The player receives three metagame options:

- save and quit;
- reload the pre-expedition save;
- begin a new career immediately in the same world.

A new career returns to employee onboarding after name entry, with appropriate previously viewed sequences optionally skippable.

Death ends this employee's career. It does not automatically retire the persistent world. The former controlled employee remains a distinct canonical person in world history as deceased, missing, unreturned, surviving, recovered, or otherwise justified by canonical state and observer knowledge.

Reloading is a player convenience outside simulation canon. It selects the sanctioned pre-expedition checkpoint and abandons the failed expedition branch. It is not resurrection within that branch, in-world time travel, or permission to merge consequences from both branches.

Player-controlled saving is permitted inside ASYNC facilities, before Complex entry, and after return. There are no ordinary player-accessible mid-expedition saves or loads.

Canonical persistence may commit whenever simulation integrity requires it, including during an expedition. Such a commit is not a player checkpoint. Invisible crash-recovery journaling may also preserve the latest trustworthy transaction, but it is not an ordinary player-selectable checkpoint or arbitrary rollback mechanism.

The five distinct state concepts are:

1. **Canonical persistence:** durable canonical history and current truth.
2. **Crash recovery:** invisible technical recovery to the latest trustworthy transaction.
3. **Session state:** ephemeral runtime/presentation state that may be reconstructed.
4. **Player checkpoint state:** a sanctioned facility, pre-entry, or post-return save boundary.
5. **Metagame reload:** abandonment of the failed branch and selection of the sanctioned pre-expedition checkpoint.

**Complex time is Complex time.**

---

## 22. Missing Personnel

Disappearance and death are different states.

A person who disappears inside the Complex is not automatically dead.

Custodian may continue representing missing personnel as persistent temporary world entities.

They may later be discovered as:

- living persons;
- drastically aged persons;
- corpses;
- skeletons carrying identifying material;
- remains altered by environmental processes such as Hay Bacillus;
- or other canon-compatible outcomes.

A later player career may encounter the remains or living continuation of a previous player character.

Subjective Complex time may differ drastically from outside time.

## 23. Threats

This section records the desired Yellow Beast behavior envelope and internal implementation vocabulary. It is not a Kane-canon admission record.

Before any named family or behavior becomes Kane-canonical production authority, `canon/SOURCE_POLICY.md` and the admitted claim/admission records must distinguish:

- behavior implemented in code;
- behavior reachable through ordinary production play;
- behavior supported by admitted primary authority;
- behavior supported only by product intent, extrapolation, fixture, or historical assumption.

Existing desired behavior must not be deleted merely because admission is incomplete. It must not be labeled Kane-canonical merely because it is desired or implemented. Custodian may retain exact internal classifications needed to simulate admitted behavior, but those classifications remain separate from player and institutional terminology.

### Still Life — Desired / Contested Behavior Envelope

The intended Still Life family is not a singular predictable monster archetype, subject to source admission and later human disposition.

Manifestations may vary radically.

They may:

- appear nearly human at first glance;
- remain motionless;
- breathe;
- fail to respond to extreme stimuli;
- scream;
- flee;
- attack;
- move rapidly;
- become partially embedded in architecture;
- interact with environmental features;
- or exhibit other canon-compatible behavior.

Their unpredictability is central.

Player-facing text should not automatically identify them canonically.

The player experiences an apparently wrong person or humanoid phenomenon unless legitimate institutional knowledge establishes otherwise.

### Bacteria — Desired / Contested Behavior Envelope

Bacteria is intended as a distinct phenomenon, subject to source admission and later human disposition.

Relevant traits may include:

- humanoid or stick-like form;
- red and dark flesh-like appearance;
- vocal mimicry;
- repetitive speech without natural tonal change;
- extreme movement;
- scratching;
- screeching;
- pursuit;
- and encounters where the entity may never be visually observed.

Again, player knowledge is not automatically internal Custodian knowledge.

## 24. Canon Authority

Kane Parsons canon is mandatory authority.

Canon admission is evidence-governed. The source registry identifies references; it does not admit them. A finished-source observation, in-fiction assertion, production artifact, secondary summary, project design statement, implemented behavior, and production-reachable behavior are different evidence classes.

Only claim-level authority admitted under `canon/SOURCE_POLICY.md` may establish Kane-canonical production behavior. Unresolved desired behavior remains explicitly desired/contested until admission or human disposition; it is neither silently promoted nor silently deleted.

Yellow Beast may generate freely inside canonical negative space.

Generated content may include:

- rooms;
- employees;
- expeditions;
- incidents;
- documents;
- institutional records;
- and unexplored architectural branches.

Generated content must not:

- contradict established canon;
- overwrite established canon;
- casually explain unresolved canonical mysteries;
- invent definitive answers to intentional unknowns;
- or convert Kane's world into generic community Backrooms lore.

Yellow Beast expands into spaces canon leaves unobserved.

It does not rewrite the observed canon to suit gameplay.

---

## 25. Unreliable Reality

The Complex may contradict human perception, maps, memories, evidence, or institutional records.

Possible events include:

- maps disagreeing with physical space;
- doors appearing or disappearing;
- teammates remembering spaces differently;
- photographs containing unexpected information;
- STANDARD possessing outdated or incorrect maps;
- returning through a doorway into a different space;
- people perceiving different aspects of the same environment;
- or other canon-compatible inconsistencies.

Custodian maintains sufficient hidden state to make consequences persistent and simulation behavior coherent.

However, Yellow Beast is not required to reduce every canonical impossibility into a conventional objective physical explanation.

Some contradiction may be an authentic property of the Complex.

---

## 26. Horror Philosophy

Less is more.

Most mundane interactions should remain mundane.

A chair may simply be a chair.

Opening a cabinet does not demand a scare.

Investigating a strange sound does not guarantee a creature encounter.

Because ordinary interactions are ordinarily ordinary, rare abnormal consequences carry greater weight.

Horror should emerge from:

- architecture;
- uncertainty;
- implication;
- distance;
- sound;
- memory;
- human behavior;
- unreliable space;
- incomplete knowledge;
- and sudden violations of previously mundane expectations.

The simulation must never behave like a haunted-house attraction attempting to entertain the player every few minutes.

---

## 27. Humor

Humor should be restrained, dry, immersive, and compatible with Kane-adjacent institutional tone and Jack's intended sensibility.

The world should rarely announce jokes.

Comedy should emerge naturally from people and bureaucracy responding seriously to absurd circumstances.

Example:

“Personnel reported sight of a table demonstrating crab-like locomotion. Claim remains unsubstantiated.”

The joke works because ASYNC is not joking.

---

## 28. Successful Mundanity

A completely uneventful mission is valid.

A team may:

- map rooms;
- perform radio checks;
- document architecture;
- improve coworker relationships;
- complete the assignment;
- return safely;
- receive a positive evaluation;
- and encounter nothing overtly paranormal.

The possibility of horror gives mundane work meaning.

Horror does not need to occur for gameplay to count.

---

## 29. Observable Simulation

Yellow Beast must regularly demonstrate that the world exists without the player.

Evidence may include:

- coworkers referencing expeditions the player never participated in;
- personnel changing jobs;
- newly discovered rooms;
- altered institutional records;
- people acting without player instruction;
- relationships evolving;
- unavailable facility rooms;
- staffing changes;
- missing personnel;
- discoveries made by others;
- or consequences originating from events the player never witnessed.

Persistence should be experienced, not merely stored.

---

## 30. Anti-Patterns

Yellow Beast is not:

- a chatbot with Backrooms decoration;
- a visual novel;
- a dialogue tree;
- a scripted quest chain;
- a click-forward narrative;
- an RPG wearing an ASYNC skin;
- a guaranteed horror-event generator;
- a generic Backrooms game;
- a game where NPCs exist only when observed;
- a game where the player controls coworkers directly;
- a game where AI prose determines reality;
- a game where the player possesses omniscient mechanical statistics;
- or a game where the universe arranges itself around the player's entertainment.

Scripted phase progression, click-forward deployment, or a sequence of ceremonial controls may be temporary historical scaffolding. None is an acceptable substitute for the intended persistent simulation, player-directed institutional navigation, or Custodian-resolved ACTION loop.

If an implementation technically functions but consistently feels like one of these things, it has failed the Yellow Beast gameplay contract.

---

## 31. Release Principle

Yellow Beast 1.0 is not defined by the smallest set of features that could technically ship.

It is also not defined by historical pass labels, completed implementation roadmaps, version strings, green partial suites, fixture demonstrations, or already-existing runtime behavior. Completion is measured against this player promise and the current vision-centric human checkpoints.

The project is intended to pursue the complete experience described by this constitution.

Scope may be implemented incrementally.

Temporary scaffolding is allowed.

Architectural compromises that permanently prevent the intended simulation are not.

**The target is the dream, not the demo.**
