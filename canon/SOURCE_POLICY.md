# Source policy

Yellow Beast separates source content, source claims, reliability, project scope,
and simulation authority. A source can support an observed fact, an in-fiction
assertion, production context, or no simulation truth at all.

## Source hierarchy

**Primary:** official finished video or film material, direct on-screen text,
direct dialogue, official supplementary releases, and clearly attributable
creator statements. These can support `observed`, `directly-stated`, or
`officially-asserted` claims, but still require claim-level classification.

**Secondary:** community transcripts, wiki summaries, published analysis, and
chronology reconstructions. They are navigation aids and may be `reference-only`;
they do not silently upgrade into authoritative simulation truth.

**Interpretive:** fan theory, community consensus, and inferred mechanisms.
These remain uncertain, scenario-optional, experimental, or prohibited until a
future explicit policy admits them.

Production material is distinct from finished-screen canon. Creator-posted
production images may document production context but are classified as
`production-artifact`, not objective simulation truth. Creator Discord material
is context-sensitive: a message is not canonical merely because a creator wrote
it. Likewise, an institutional statement inside the fiction is not automatically
objective truth; it may be modeled as an institutional assertion or character
belief instead.

This repository stores source metadata and original analytical claims, never
transcripts, film frames, production artwork, or Discord archives.

## Admission control

The registry identifies a reference; it does not admit it. The intake lifecycle
and reviewer requirements are defined in [Verified source intake](../docs/intake-workflow.md).
A claim may influence objective scenario state only when its reviewed authority
allows that exact use. Source classification, claim extraction, canon review,
and scenario admission are separate records.
